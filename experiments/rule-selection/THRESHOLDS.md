# Four thresholds on the development inputs

To run it again, from the repository's root:

```bash
cd experiments/rule-selection && /Users/ak/Documents/tensrview/tensrviewproject/.venv/bin/python measure_thresholds.py
```

It prints every table below in full, one row per record set, and writes the same numbers to `generated/thresholds.json` (ignored). It takes about two minutes. If the two toy captures are missing, it makes them first with the allowlist's own commands, offline, into `generated/`. A checkout without the TrainCheck sweep in `experiments/traincheck-bugs/out/` lists the `traincheck` entry as not read. Add `--traincheck-from <a checkout that has it>` to read it there.

Wave AE, lane AE2, Nov 21, 2026, at `265311c`. This is a measurement only: no rule, threshold or detector was changed, and nothing here is a proposal. Every input came through `inputs.py` from `allowlist.json`. Gate 1's six held-out cases and their golden copies were not read.

## What was read

**69 record sets from all 15 entries** that `everything()` covers. Two of them needed something extra:

- **`traincheck`** reads from the worktree, and this lane's worktree holds no sweep. Its 60 record sets were read from the main checkout's `experiments/traincheck-bugs/out/` (written Nov 21, 13:49), through the loader's own glob and refused words, with `--traincheck-from`. `RESULTS.md` counted 46 TrainCheck record sets. This folder holds 60, among them the `-probe` and `-pytest` configurations. So the TrainCheck sets here are not exactly the ones wave AD read.
- **`toy-moe` and `toy-seeded-healthy`** existed nowhere. They were generated by the script with the allowlist's commands. Both fix their seed (`torch.manual_seed(0)`, and a seeded token generator), but a rerun was not compared byte for byte.

The seeded-failure toy's `grad_spike` and `stalled_weights` cases are not on the allowlist, so they were not run. The fault columns for those two rules rest on one hand-written record set and a few TrainCheck bugs.

**How each line was varied.** The detector's own code was used throughout.

| Rule | How |
|---|---|
| Total spike | `detect_grad_spikes(factor=f)`, with `sustain` set out of reach so that only the total's rule fires. The largest ratio comes from a copy of the total's ratio. It agreed with the detector's `spike_count` and `spike_steps` at 3, 4, 5 and 8 times on every record set. |
| Parameter spike | `detect_grad_spikes(factor=f, sustain=s)` on copies of the records whose finite totals are all set to 1.0, so only the parameter rule can fire. The longest streak is the largest `s` that fires. |
| Diversity drop | No argument takes the line. `evidence.DIVERSITY_DROP` was set for the call to `_moe_samples` and put back afterwards. Every sample's `overload_rule["drop"]` held the line it was set to. The closest a router came was read by wrapping `_jump_overloaded`, which sees the router's own window. |
| Starved experts | `_mark_starved(samples, window, ratio)`. |
| Stalled weights | `detect_stalled_weights(steps=n)`. The longest streak is the largest `n` that fires. |

The only copy of a rule is the total's ratio, and it matched the detector everywhere. At today's lines, each record set's four results were compared with `build_evidence`'s incidents: 73 record sets (the 69, plus Granite's part before its collapse), and none differ.

**What counts as healthy.**

- The sets the allowlist calls healthy.
- The part of each seeded set before its fault: Granite before step 450 for all four rules, SmolLM2 and both `Trainer` runs before 250, and `toy_moe` before 200.
- nanoMoE without its balance loss, for every rule but starvation (the allowlist: "starved experts expected, nothing else").

Which seeded set carries each rule's own fault was read from the allowlist's notes, `experiments/traincheck-bugs/catalog.py` and the hand-written sets' source, never from a run's output.

## How far this goes

- **The healthy sets are few workloads.** Four Granite sets are one job in four capture modes. The two nanoMoE arms are one model and one seed. Twelve TrainCheck healthy sets are one model shared by B9, B10 and the hardware simulation. Ranks and configurations of one script are one workload.

  | Rule | Healthy sets judged | Distinct workloads |
  |---|---|---|
  | Gradient spike | 33 of 38 | 12 |
  | Diversity drop | 4 of 38 | 1 (Granite) |
  | Starved experts | 6 of 37 | 3 (Granite, nanoMoE, `toy_moe`) |
  | Stalled weights | 26 of 38 | 10 |

- **The rules were fitted to Granite.** `SPIKE_SUSTAIN` was chosen on Granite captures, and `DIVERSITY_DROP` cites Granite's healthy 0.82 and its collapse at 0.29. Every quiet Granite result below is a fit, not a test.
- **The hand-written sets were written to fit today's rules.** One example: w1's two-check streak was planted so that three checks do not fire.
- **The other runs were not used to set these four lines, but they were read in wave AD** to choose other rules. They are mostly short (200 steps or fewer, with three weight checks), so a quiet result there says little.
- **Nothing here is a large model trained from scratch, and no run is longer than 2,001 steps.**

## 4. Stalled weights: unchanged on 3 weight checks in a row

**Healthy.** 26 sets judged, from 10 workloads. Not judged:

- 9 sets with no weight check in the capture: Granite's no-gradient and routers-only captures, B1's two 22-step runs, B8, B10's 30-step runs, and `toy_moe`.
- 3 sets with fewer than 3 checks: B4 (1) and B14 (2 each).

Checks run every 50 steps by default, so a 200-step TrainCheck run has three. At 3 checks, only its last check can complete a streak, and at 5 checks no TrainCheck run can be judged.

| Healthy set | Weight checks | Longest streak | 2 checks | 3 (today) | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Granite before 450, full and default captures | 8 | **8** | Fires at 149 (28 parameters) | **Fires at 199 (28)** | Fires at 299 (28) |
| SmolLM2 before 250 | 4 | **4** | Fires at 149 (28) | **Fires at 199 (28)** | Not judged (4 checks) |
| `Trainer`, both runs, before 250 | 4 | 0 | — | — | — |
| nanoMoE, both arms | 39 | 0 | — | — | — |
| Seeded-failure toy | 6 | 0 | — | — | — |
| The 18 TrainCheck sets judged | 3 | 0 | — | — | — |

- **Two GPU fine-tunes fire at every line they can be judged at:** Granite and SmolLM2, before their faults.
- **On Granite the 28 parameters are layer-norm weights** (`model.layers.N.input_layernorm.weight` and `post_attention_layernorm.weight`). Not one was seen changing at any check (`trained_before` 0). The rule's own docstring names this case: bf16 weights near 1.0, where an update smaller than the weight's spacing rounds away. These records hold no `weight_dtypes`, so the capture cannot confirm the precision.
- **The streak lasts every check of the run** (8 of 8 on Granite, 4 of 4 on SmolLM2), so no stall count separates it.
- **Every other healthy set has a longest streak of 0.**

**Other seeded sets that fire:**

- Granite's full run fires at 149, 199 and 299, the same parameters as its healthy part.
- SmolLM2's full run fires at 149 (60 parameters), 199 (32) and 299 (28).
- No TrainCheck bug other than B1 has a streak.

## What the numbers show, rule by rule

- **Gradient spike.**
  - At 5× no healthy set fires; at 4× three do, at 3× five do.
  - The parameter rule's sustain of 3 is quiet on every healthy set; a sustain of 2 fires on two.
  - The one TrainCheck spike fault (B4) is missed at every line, because its rise is over before the rule has history.
- **Diversity drop.**
  - Quiet on its one healthy job, and every seeded drop fires at 0.5, 0.6 and 0.7.
  - That is one job, the one the line was set on.
- **Starved experts.**
  - Fires on healthy Granite at every ratio and window tried.
  - Quiet on nanoMoE and `toy_moe` with the balance loss at every line.
  - The two seeded router collapses are not starvation by this rule's definition: their experts go dead, and `moe_imbalance` reports them.
- **Stalled weights.**
  - Fires at every count on two healthy GPU fine-tunes, on layer-norm weights never seen changing.
  - Quiet everywhere else.
  - The TrainCheck captures are too short to judge counts above 3.
